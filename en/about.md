<div class="content margem-desktop">

  <p class="is-size-5">
    I'm a data scientist with a PhD in Political Science from the University of São Paulo (USP). I've worked with quantitative methods since my master's, and almost everything I've done since then comes back to the same concern: knowing what a number can support and what it can't. In practice, that means thinking about the sample before computing the indicator, and separating the effect of a decision from a mere association in the data before recommending anything.
  </p>

  <p class="is-size-5">
    These days I split my time between Cetic.br and consulting.
  </p>

  <h4 class="title is-4"><i class="fas fa-chart-bar"></i> At Cetic.br</h4>

  <p class="is-size-5">
    Since March 2022, I've been a data scientist and researcher at <strong>Cetic.br</strong>, the NIC.br center that produces Brazil's national surveys and indicators on internet and technology use. My work there is methodological innovation, which covers sampling design, weighting, and the analysis of large-scale surveys.
  </p>

  <p class="is-size-5">
    I rebuilt an internal processing routine, adding functions for different sampling designs so it could be reused across all of the center's surveys. I validated and implemented the weighting methods that make a sample of SIMET schools (Escola Conectada) representative of all municipal and state schools in Brazil. I also applied NLP, with text classification and analysis, to open-ended responses from three online panel projects on privacy and e-waste; two of them have results published on the Cetic.br website.
  </p>

  <p class="is-size-5">
    Another part of the job is less visible but matters day to day: an internal tabulation tool used by managers and analysts, tracking of deliverables from data collection suppliers, and automation of processing steps, which cut manual work in producing tables and dashboards.
  </p>

  <h4 class="title is-4"><i class="fas fa-briefcase"></i> Consulting</h4>

  <p class="is-size-5">
    Since 2020, I've worked with organizations that need statistical modeling, research design, or automated analysis in R and Python. Most projects involve <strong>causal inference</strong>. When it's possible to randomize who receives an intervention, I design and analyze randomized controlled trials; when it isn't, I turn to quasi-experimental designs (difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity, propensity score matching, instrumental variables), chosen according to what the data can actually support. The goal is to estimate the effect of a policy, a behavioral intervention, or a product decision, not just to describe what happened alongside it.
  </p>

  <h4 class="title is-4"><i class="fas fa-graduation-cap"></i> Academia</h4>

  <p class="is-size-5">
    I earned a bachelor's in International Relations at Unesp (2007–2012), then a master's and a PhD in Political Science at USP. During the master's, funded by a CAPES fellowship, I studied the effectiveness of internationalization policies by subnational governments, combining statistical models with case study analysis. A paper from that period, estimating causal effects, was presented at the Midwest Political Science Association conference.
  </p>

  <p class="is-size-5">
    My PhD, funded by CNPq, looked at the impact of artificial intelligence on the labor market and income inequality in Brazil. I used time series and <em>machine learning</em> on microdata to understand which occupations face the greatest potential impact and what that would mean for income distribution. The expected impact on the employment level is not negative, although a causal relationship with the features of the Brazilian formal labor market cannot be established. During that time I completed three editions of the IPSA (International Political Science Association) methods summer school, with funding from USP's Department of Political Science.
  </p>

  <p class="is-size-5">
    In 2026, I took the MBA in Artificial Intelligence and Analytics Applied to Business at FGV.
  </p>

  <h4 class="title is-4"><i class="fas fa-users"></i> During the pandemic</h4>

  <p class="is-size-5">
    From April to September 2020, I was part of the task force of the Solidarity Research Network (Rede de Pesquisa Solidária), which published analyses of the pandemic's socioeconomic effects using microdata from IBGE's PNAD Contínua and PNAD Covid surveys. I contributed an expanded unemployment measure, "hidden unemployment", which counts the discouraged workers that standard metrics leave out; simulations of the poverty rate without the R$600 Emergency Aid; and analyses of internet access, showing how lack of connectivity made it harder both to receive the aid and to work remotely.
  </p>

  <h4 class="title is-4"><i class="fas fa-chalkboard-teacher"></i> Teaching</h4>

  <p class="is-size-5">
    From 2018 to 2023, I taught data methods courses at Cebrap for professionals and researchers, including R programming, focused on data structures and advanced analysis, and web data collection with R.
  </p>
